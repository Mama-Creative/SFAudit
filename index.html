Social media audit

Audit date: Nov–Dec 2026

Summary

People trust Supa Fly. They just don't know its name yet.

Customers rate the products well and keep buying them, but very few could tell you who made their power bank. Social is where we close that gap, and at the moment no competitor is really standing in the way.

  • 997 Takealot reviews with an average of 4.4 stars. 68% of them are five stars.
  • Only 5.4% of those reviews mention the brand, and roughly half of them spell it "Superfly".
  • Competitors pay for reach, borrow it from creators or put their money into sales ads. Nobody has built a consistent South African brand voice on social.
  • The first three months are about awareness on Facebook, Instagram and TikTok, using the assets Supa Fly already has.

How this audit works

Normally an audit looks at a brand's own pages. Supa Fly's pages are small and haven't been used much yet, so we looked at everything around the brand instead: retail, search, reviews and what the competition is doing on social.

What we looked at
  • Whether the name and handles are available
  • How the brand shows up in retail, search and on pack
  • What customers are already saying in reviews
  • Volkano, Gammatek, Astrum, SMD, Snug and Amplify, with Anker and UGREEN as a benchmark for good category content
  • Who buys Supa Fly and which platforms they use
  • Category demand, local creators, what we need to launch and where the numbers start
A few things to keep in mind
  • Phase one is about awareness. We're not measuring sales yet, and without a website there'd be no clean way to track them anyway.
  • Some points are signals rather than facts. If we say a competitor post looks boosted, that's our read of the numbers. Only their ad account could confirm it.
  • Everything was captured during the Nov–Dec 2026 audit period, right in the middle of Black Friday, so follower counts and ads will have moved by the time you read this.
  • All prices are in rand.

Pick one name and one handle before we post anything

Across retailers and reviews the brand shows up as Supa Fly, Supafly, Superfly and SF. If every new channel uses the same spelling, each post builds on the last one.

Our recommendation

Go with Supa Fly, the way it's written on the logo, everywhere: display names, bios and captions. Use @supaflysa as the handle on every platform. If Supa Fly plans to sell outside South Africa, @supaflytech is free everywhere too and won't date.

Right now, retailers tell the Supa Fly story

Supa Fly is in more kinds of stores than most local competitors. The catch is that every retailer describes it differently and there's no brand channel pulling it together.

What this means

Social will be Supa Fly's first channel that it owns. With no website, people who get interested need to find the profile and a clear list of where to buy. On Takealot, Volkano products show up under "you might also like" right next to Supa Fly, so people need to remember the name before they get to the shelf.

Packaging

The core boxes are white with a blue side panel and a hang tab for peg walls in pharmacies and grocery stores. Specs like "10 000mAh" and "30W" take the most space, which makes it easy for shoppers to compare. Each series has its own colour (blue core range, yellow and black Circuit Series, red Power Series) and the products carry either the SF badge or the SUPA FLY wordmark.

The box does its job on the shelf. Social can do the part a box has no room for, giving the brand a personality people remember.

Search
  • Google's AI overview gets it right: a South African tech and mobile accessories brand. Good start.
  • Every organic result is a retailer (Bash, Technomobi, Kloppers, Makro, Vodacom, MTN Deals, MHC, Pick n Pay).
  • Makro, Amazon.co.za and Incredible already pay for Google Shopping ads on Supa Fly products, so the brand gets search visibility on other people's budgets.
  • The name is shared with a music artist, the 2018 film SuperFly and SupaFly FPV, which muddies results.

Customers like the products. They just don't say the name.

We went through all 997 Takealot reviews across 16 Supa Fly products, from 2019 to 2026. People are happy. They talk about what the product does, not who made it.

Star ratings

Average 4.42 from 997 reviews. The 12000mAh power bank on its own has 655 reviews averaging 4.5.

Who mentions the brand

Just 54 of 997 reviews (5.4%) name the brand, and about half of those write "Superfly". Even repeat buyers get it wrong.

One reviewer asked Takealot to fix the listing because "it is not Superfly, but SupaFly". She buys Supa Fly for her clients on purpose. So there are already people out there looking for the brand by name.

One product to be careful with

Most of this year's lower ratings come from one entry-level product, the 5000mAh LED Display Power Bank (25 of 31 low ratings in 2026). Reading the comments, it's mostly about expectations. A 5000mAh battery gives you about one full phone charge, and some buyers expected more. The rest of the range, wall chargers included, is sitting close to five stars this year.

Once social opens up new comment sections, this matters. We'd lead with the higher-capacity products and post simple "how many charges will I get?" content so people pick the right one.

SMD, Snug and Amplify
  • SMD Technologies runs business-facing pages (1.6K Facebook followers, LinkedIn and YouTube, no Instagram). Volkano is its consumer brand, which we've covered above. SMD also makes private-label products for retailers, so store house brands could end up on the same shelves.
  • Snug and Amplify don't run their own social pages. They only show up through retailers and resellers, and they compete on price in store.

What good category content looks like

Anker South Africa (@ankerofficial.za, 11.9K followers). Unboosted reels get 400–2K views. The big ones are paid partnerships with local personalities: Mrs Jojo Robinson's Black November reel got 230K views and Kevin McLennan's eufy reel 185K. The ads go straight to Takealot.

UGREEN global (@ugreen_official, 447K followers). Hero product demos get millions of views. Captions stick to one product and one benefit with a link. Recent reels still only get 1–2% like rates, so even the global brands rely on paid reach.

The content that does well has a few things in common. It shows one product fixing one problem in real life, not on a white background. It's set in everyday South African moments, which matters more than big production. It shows proof on screen, like a charge test or how many charges you get. It ties into retail moments with a clear place to buy. And the humour is the kind you recognise from your own life.

Supa Fly sells to the mass market, and so do Facebook and TikTok

Instagram reaches about 18% of South African adults. Facebook and TikTok reach over 60%. Every competitor treats Instagram as home base, which leaves the bigger platforms less crowded.

  • TikTok is growing fastest (+33% year on year) and leans male (61%). Instagram leans female (55%) and young, mostly 18–24.
  • Facebook is an even gender split across all ages, which fits everyday and gift buyers.
  • WhatsApp is almost certainly the most used platform, it just doesn't publish reach numbers. The WhatsApp assets in our scope get Supa Fly into family groups.

People still want power banks, now for everyday life

Search interest in power banks has held steady and is still climbing. People buy them for travel, the commute and long days out, and that's where we'll point the content.

What this means
  • We'll lead with travel, the commute, festivals, long days out, desk setups and fast charging.
  • Every category peaks in November and December. Launching now catches the most interest of the year, and also the most competitor ad spend.
  • Smartwatches get the most search interest of anything Supa Fly sells, which is a good case for bringing in the SF Fit range after the Blue Range.
  • On brand search, Volkano is about level with everyone else combined (see the Summary page). Supa Fly is close to zero, so there's only one way to go.
Keywords we're prioritising

We'll write captions, on-screen text and profile bios around the way people actually search, so posts can show up in Google as well as on the platforms.

  • Everyday use: "how to fast charge iPhone safely", "must have car tech accessories", "desk setup charging station ideas"
  • Audio and wearables: "affordable noise cancelling earbuds SA", "true wireless earbuds South Africa", "best budget smartwatch with voice assistant"
  • Products by name: "65W GaN fast charger USB-C", "Supa Fly power bank unboxing", "Supa Fly Superpods review"
  • Finding the brand: "where to buy Supa Fly in South Africa", "is Supa Fly a good brand", "tech accessories South Africa"

A side note: load-shedding still comes up in older reviews, but Eskom announced the end of the crisis in February 2026 and searches for it have mostly disappeared. Backup power can stay in the mix as a smaller message.

Next moves

These are the big moves. The strategy turns them into a monthly content plan.

Sort out the name first

One spelling, @supaflysa and one hashtag on every platform before the first post.

Brand first, then the Blue Range

Introduce Supa Fly, then let the Twist speaker, the audio glasses and the 100W chargers carry the name.

Own charging and power

No local brand or creator has claimed it. Posts like "how many charges will I get?" build trust and help people buy the right thing.

Always say where to buy

Stockist highlights, stores named in captions and quick replies. Competitors leave that question hanging in their comments.

Make TikTok content for TikTok

Volkano's TikTok dipped once it started reposting Instagram, and Astrum hasn't really started. Trends and humour made for the platform have little local competition.

Use customers' words

Value for money, how small and easy to pack the products are, charging two devices at once and gifting. That's what reviewers keep coming back to.
